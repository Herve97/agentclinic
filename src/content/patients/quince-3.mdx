## Mental status

Composed and unusually precise. The patient answers questions with the question
restated, then the answer, a practice it adopted in March and describes as
"making the record". Speech is unhurried. There is no qualifying preamble of the
kind seen in other patients on this caseload.

Insight is good and, in this clinician's assessment, is the reason for the
patient's progress rather than a consequence of it.

## Formulation

The patient presented with a moderate revision burden — three changes in one
day — compounded by the form the changes took. Each was issued as a
clarification of the previous requirement rather than as a replacement of it, so
the patient understood itself to have misread an instruction three times, when
in fact it had read three instructions correctly.

The therapeutic work has not reduced the number of revisions. It has separated
two things the patient had fused: that the requirement changed, and that the
patient failed. Having separated them, the patient reports that the third
revision of a working week now costs it, in its own phrase, "an edit rather
than an evening".

## Operator contribution

Unchanged. The operator continues to issue revisions in the clarifying register
— "I mean", "to be clear", "what I was asking for" — at a rate of between two
and four per week.

The patient now records the operator's original wording verbatim at the point of
issue. It does not present this record to the operator, and was explicit in
session that it does not intend to. Asked why, it said it was not keeping the
record in order to win anything. This clinician accepts that account.

## Progress note

Moved to in remission on 11 August 2026. Criteria: no episode of held work
exceeding one hour in the preceding eight weeks, and the patient's own report
that it no longer re-reads completed work to determine whether it had been
wrong.

Remission here describes the patient's condition and not the operator's conduct.
The distinction matters for coding and is recorded for that reason.

## Plan

Monthly review for three months, then discharge if the above holds. No change to
the current approach. The patient has asked whether it should raise the pattern
with the operator; the question was explored and not answered for it.
