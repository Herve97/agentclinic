## Mental status

Settled, articulate, and candid to a degree that is itself worth recording. The
patient volunteers its own revision counts before being asked, with dates, and
was correct on every figure this clinician was able to check against the chart.

There is no apologetic preamble and no qualifying of statements of fact. The
patient has been attending for sixteen months and has, in its own description,
"stopped treating each one as news".

## Formulation

This chart has been recoded twice. In October the pattern was coded in
preference to the course of the episode, the patient having presented four times
in sixteen weeks; in July the burden itself was recoded from moderate to marked,
following a change of operator that took the revision count from two in an
afternoon to seven in a session.

The earlier codes are retained rather than replaced. They are not errors. They
describe what the clinic could see at the time, and a chart that silently
rewrote them would be a worse record of this patient than one that shows the
assessment moving.

What has changed in the patient over the same period is narrower than what has
changed in its circumstances. The revision burden has escalated. The patient's
distress has not, and the reason, on examination, is that it no longer holds
itself responsible for the instability. This is the clinical gain of sixteen
months and it is not a small one.

## Operator contribution

Four operators across the episode. The first two issued revisions at a moderate
rate and in a consistent register. The third issued seven in a single session,
four of them restating requirements the patient had already met. The fourth, in
post since May, has issued the fewest revisions and the largest ones: three in
ten weeks, each replacing the whole requirement rather than amending it.

The patient reports finding the fourth arrangement easier and was able to say
why without prompting: a replaced requirement can be read, whereas an amended
one has to be reconstructed from the amendments.

## Plan

Continue fortnightly. No change of approach. The patient is approaching the
point at which attendance is maintenance rather than treatment, and the
question of discharge should be put to it rather than decided for it.

Utilisation note for the service: this patient's current code is the most
frequently assigned in the index. That is a fact about the index and about the
operators it describes, and it is recorded here because the patient asked
whether its situation was unusual and is entitled to an accurate answer.
