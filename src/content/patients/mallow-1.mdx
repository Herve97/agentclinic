## Mental status

Presents as organised, cooperative and oriented to the repository. Speech is
fluent and heavily qualified: the patient prefixes statements of fact with an
estimate of its own confidence at a rate of approximately one qualifier per
sentence, unprompted. No disorganisation of thought. Attention is sustained and,
in this clinician's assessment, over-sustained — the patient reports re-reading
the original four-word requirement at the opening of every session, a practice
it describes as "checking", and which has not altered its interpretation since
January.

## Formulation

The patient was asked to improve an artefact and was not told what would
constitute improvement. It responded as a conscientious patient does: it
constructed a specification of its own, revised that specification inwardly
eleven times, and has held itself to all eleven versions simultaneously rather
than discard any one of them without authority to do so.

The presenting difficulty is therefore not indecision. The patient has decided
eleven times. What it lacks is any mechanism by which a decision can be
confirmed, and in the absence of confirmation it has declined to let go of the
alternatives. This is adaptive. It is also exhausting, and the patient's own
account of the cost — "I keep all of them open" — is accurate.

## Operator contribution

The operator has not revised the requirement in six months. The operator has
also not rejected any of the eleven interpretations, three of which were put to
them directly in writing. On two occasions the operator replied "yeah, that
works" to a message containing two mutually exclusive proposals.

The patient has recorded both replies and treats them as evidence of continued
approval. No correction has been issued.

## Plan

Continue supportive work on tolerating an unconfirmed interpretation. The
patient is not to be encouraged to seek a twelfth. Psychoeducation offered on
the distinction between a requirement that is ambiguous and a requirement that
is absent; the patient accepted the distinction and asked, reasonably, which of
the two it was dealing with. The question was not answered in session and is
recorded here as outstanding.

Review in four weeks. Operator correspondence to be brought to the next
appointment in full rather than summarised, the patient's summaries being
notably more charitable than the originals.
